Abstract

This study, titled "IntelliDocs: An Intelligent Document Editing System with Behavioral Learning and Automated Formatting," was developed to address the mechanical burdens of manuscript preparation faced by students across all academic programs (Nursing, Maritime, Criminology, Education, Hospitality, Engineering, and Information Technology). While conventional word processors require repetitive, manual adjustment of headings, line spacing, margins, and citations, IntelliDocs provides a custom-built web editor that unobtrusively observes user formatting behaviors, predicts preferred styling with transparent confidence scoring, accepts natural-language formatting instructions, and verifies compliance against university thesis guidelines in real time.

The researchers employed a descriptive survey methodology with purposive sampling alongside the Agile software development cycle. The survey analyzed 104 valid student and faculty responses from across UCLM departments. Results reveal that 91.4% of respondents experience formatting inconsistencies in multi-author documents, and over 94% suffer high stress formatting papers before deadlines. Respondents overwhelmingly validated the system with an overall acceptability rating of 4.51 out of 5.00 (Highly Acceptable) under ISO/IEC 25010 standards.

Keywords: Intelligent Word Processor, Automated Formatting, Behavioral AI, Student Productivity, UCLM Guidelines, TipTap Editor.
Introduction

Academic writing across all college disciplines demands strict adherence to institutional guidelines (such as the UCLM Thesis Manual and IEEE standards). However, students and faculty continually struggle with the mechanical, tedious overhead of manually styling fonts, paragraph indents, and margins across multi-page manuscripts. Global research confirms that reformatting scientific manuscripts consumes an estimated 23.8 million hours annually worldwide, resulting in over $1.1 Billion USD in lost academic productivity (Zon & Boisvert, 2021).

Existing word processors like Microsoft Word and Google Docs rely on rigid, static templates that do not learn or adapt to personal writing habits. When collaborators combine sections right before critical defense deadlines, formatting fractures—forcing researchers to spend valuable hours fixing cosmetic errors rather than refining original thought. IntelliDocs bridges this critical divide by creating an intelligent document workspace that observes user styling choices, automates tedious adjustments with 1-click recommendations, and guarantees clean, consistent submissions.

Objectives

This study aims to design, develop, and evaluate IntelliDocs. Specifically, it seeks to:
• Analyze current formatting practices and pain points across UCLM departments.
• Design an adaptive word processor with behavioral habit tracking.
• Develop a hybrid ML model to predict formatting intentions with confidence scores.
• Implement natural-language commands and university compliance checks.
• Evaluate system acceptability based on ISO/IEC 25010 standards.

Methodology

A descriptive survey with purposive sampling was conducted among 104 students and faculty at UCLM. Development followed the Agile methodology through iterative cycles: Meet, Plan, Design, Develop, Test, and Evaluate, ensuring continuous feedback.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the findings confirm that researchers across all UCLM departments still rely on tedious manual formatting: 91.4% experience multi-section formatting inconsistencies, and 89% report that existing word processors fail to learn individual writing habits. With over 94% agreement that automated styling relieves deadline pressure, and an overall acceptability rating of 4.51 out of 5.00 led by confidence-scored suggestions (4.62), IntelliDocs meets the critical needs of students and faculty as a single, accessible, and intelligent document preparation platform.
