IntelliDocs
An Intelligent Document Editing System with Behavioral Learning and Automated Formatting
College of Computer Studies • University of Cebu – Lapu-Lapu and Mandaue (UCLM)
Abstract

This study introduces IntelliDocs, an intelligent web-based document editing system with behavioral machine learning and automated formatting designed for academic researchers across all degree programs (Nursing, Maritime, Criminology, Education, Hospitality, Engineering, and Information Technology). While conventional word processors require repetitive, manual adjustment of styles, line spacing, margins, and citations, IntelliDocs provides a custom editor that learns user formatting habits in real time, generates confidence-scored recommendations, accepts natural-language commands, and verifies compliance against university thesis guidelines.

A descriptive survey with purposive sampling conducted across UCLM colleges revealed that 91.4% of respondents experience multi-section formatting inconsistencies and over 94% suffer severe stress preparing manuscripts before deadlines. System evaluation under ISO/IEC 25010 standards achieved an overall acceptability score of 4.51 out of 5.00 (Highly Acceptable).

Keywords: Intelligent Word Processor, Automated Formatting, Behavioral AI, Student Productivity, UCLM Guidelines.
Introduction

Formatting research manuscripts to institutional standards is one of the most tedious hurdles faced by college researchers. Scientific publication studies reveal that manual manuscript reformatting consumes an estimated 23.8 million hours annually worldwide, resulting in over $1.1 Billion USD in lost academic productivity (Zon & Boisvert, 2021).

Current word processors rely on static templates that fail to learn individual typing habits. When multi-author sections are merged before defense deadlines, formatting breaks—forcing students to spend hours fixing cosmetic styling rather than refining original thought. IntelliDocs bridges this gap by learning user preferences, automating adjustments with 1-click recommendations, and enforcing institutional compliance.

Objectives

This study aims to design, develop, and evaluate IntelliDocs. Specifically, it seeks to:

• Analyze: Current formatting practices, inconsistencies, and manual overhead across academic departments.
• Design: An adaptive word processing interface with non-intrusive behavioral habit logging.
• Develop: A hybrid ML model to predict formatting intentions with transparent confidence scoring.
• Implement: Natural-language formatting commands and UCLM institutional guideline validation.
• Evaluate: System acceptability and usability based on ISO/IEC 25010 software quality standards.
Methodology

A descriptive survey design with purposive sampling was conducted among student researchers and faculty members across diverse degree programs at UCLM. System development followed the Agile software lifecycle, cycling through six iterative stages: Plan, Design, Develop, Test, Release, and Feedback. Continuous evaluations were performed using the ISO/IEC 25010 software quality framework.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the findings confirm that researchers across all UCLM departments still rely on tedious manual formatting: 91.4% experience multi-section formatting inconsistencies, and 89% report that existing word processors fail to learn individual writing habits. With over 94% agreement that automated styling relieves deadline pressure, and an overall acceptability rating of 4.51 out of 5.00 led by confidence-scored suggestions (4.62), IntelliDocs meets the critical needs of students and faculty as a single, accessible, and intelligent document preparation platform.
